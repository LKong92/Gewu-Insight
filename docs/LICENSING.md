## Public source is not an open-source license

The research-only field-of-use limit means Gewu is not offered as open-source
software under the [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd).
Use "source-available research software", or "publicly available source code"
when describing code that has actually been published. Do not label this custom
license MIT, Apache, GPL or another standard license.

Public GitHub repositories permit viewing and forking under
[GitHub's platform terms](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository).
Those rights do not create permission to commercially use Gewu. Changing the
repository's visibility is a separate publication decision; installing this
license does not change GitHub settings.

**Setting → Literature Data Source** configures scholarly discovery and metadata
services. Those services retain their own access policies and terms.

## Future contributions and commercial releases

The authors retain the right to license code they own or are authorized to
license commercially. A pull request under the research license does not
automatically provide additional commercial sublicensing or relicensing rights.
Before merging external contributions, maintainers must confirm the contributor's
authority and obtain the additional written grant needed for the project's
commercial licensing model. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

This merge requirement does not restrict research users from independently
publishing modifications permitted by LICENSE. No finalized CLA, signature
collection process or commercial grant from an external contributor is currently
established by these documents. The intended agreement should preserve contributor
copyright while expressly addressing commercial use, sublicensing, relicensing
and any intended transfer to a future project entity. It needs legal review and
actual agreement; submitting a PR is not treated as signing it.

### Contribution authorization checklist

This is a maintainer checklist for preparing a separate agreement, not a CLA or
evidence that a contributor has signed one.

Maintainers should document:

- The contribution and the person or organization authorized to license it,
  including any relevant employer or third-party rights.
- The exact signed agreement and contributions it covers, with records retained
  privately rather than committed to the public repository.
- The permission to use, reproduce, modify and distribute the contribution and
  derivative works, including commercial use and sublicensing/relicensing under
  the project's intended research and commercial terms.
- Whether the grant is perpetual and irrevocable, its conditions, and whether it
  permits an intended transfer to a future project entity or successor. A future
  company must not be assumed to acquire rights automatically.
- Patent permissions where relevant, third-party notices and provenance.

A sign-off, Git author name or PR submission alone is not evidence of the
additional commercial grant. Keep actual agreements and signatures private.
[GitHub Terms of Service, D.6](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service#6-contributions-under-repository-license)
describes contributions under a repository's license and the role of a separate
agreement; the inbound research license does not supply broader commercial
relicensing rights.
